ABOUT

Research profile

Dr. Eric Bastos Görgens is a Forest Engineer, Professor and Research Scientist at the Department of Forest Engineering of the Federal University of the Jequitinhonha and Mucuri Valleys (UFVJM), Brazil. His scientific career is strongly focused on forest management, precision forestry, forest monitoring, environmental zoning, remote sensing and the integration of computational methods into forest science.

He is a CNPq Research Productivity Fellow (Level 2) and has developed a distinguished academic and professional trajectory connecting forest inventory, LiDAR, artificial intelligence, geospatial technologies and sustainable forest management. His research contributes to the development of methods and decision-support systems for monitoring, planning and managing forest ecosystems with high spatial precision.

Within GigaFlor and PELD-MIAG, Dr. Görgens contributes scientific leadership in remote sensing, airborne LiDAR, giant-tree detection, environmental modelling and large-scale forest monitoring. His expertise strengthens the observatory’s capacity to integrate field inventories with airborne and satellite remote sensing technologies to understand the distribution, ecology, vulnerability and conservation of giant Amazonian trees.

GIANT AMAZON TREES

Pioneering the discovery of Amazon giant trees

Dr. Görgens is internationally recognized as one of the pioneering scientists involved in the discovery, mapping and scientific documentation of the tallest trees in the Brazilian Amazon. Through the application of airborne LiDAR technology and advanced spatial modelling, he played a central role in identifying emergent giant trees across the Amazon Basin, including some of the tallest tropical trees ever recorded in South America.

His research helped reveal the ecological significance, spatial distribution and conservation importance of giant tropical forests, contributing substantially to global scientific understanding of these exceptional ecosystems. These discoveries changed how scientists perceive the vertical structure of Amazonian forests and opened new research frontiers on the ecology, biodiversity, biomass and climate sensitivity of giant trees.

In the context of GigaFlor, his work provides a key foundation for linking airborne LiDAR surveys, spatial ecology and field expeditions aimed at understanding why giant trees occur in specific regions, how they shape forest carbon stocks and how vulnerable they may be under increasing climatic stress.

SCIENTIFIC APPROACH

LiDAR, artificial intelligence and precision forest management

Dr. Görgens has been among the pioneers in applying airborne LiDAR to tropical forest monitoring in Brazil. His doctoral research focused on the quantification and qualification of vegetation cover using LiDAR technology, and his subsequent work has expanded the use of laser scanning, remote sensing and geospatial modelling for forest biomass estimation, canopy structure characterization and spatially explicit forest management.

His scientific expertise spans tropical forest ecology, forest mensuration, airborne laser scanning, artificial intelligence, machine learning, environmental zoning, precision forestry and ecosystem monitoring. His work bridges field ecology and advanced remote sensing approaches to better understand forest structure, biomass, biodiversity, carbon dynamics and ecosystem resilience under climate change.

By integrating forest inventory, remote sensing from UAVs, aircraft and satellites, spatial statistics, programming, optimization and machine learning, his research supports new approaches for monitoring and managing natural resources and landscapes across multiple scales.

GIGAFLOR CONTRIBUTION

Spatial intelligence for giant forest observatories

In the GigaFlor network, Dr. Görgens contributes to the development of a spatially explicit scientific framework for detecting, mapping and monitoring giant Amazonian trees. His expertise is central to transforming airborne LiDAR point clouds, canopy height models, field inventories and environmental layers into products that support ecological modelling, conservation planning and long-term monitoring.

His contribution helps connect the discovery of exceptional trees with broader questions about forest structure, biomass distribution, sustainable management, environmental constraints and climate resilience. This perspective is essential for building a next-generation observatory capable of linking field-based forest ecology with advanced geospatial technologies.

Beyond his scientific contributions, Dr. Görgens is strongly engaged in graduate training and interdisciplinary research, promoting innovation at the interface of forest ecology, computational science, remote sensing and environmental management.

FIELDWORK AND TECHNOLOGY

From LiDAR transects to forest conservation

Dr. Görgens’ work combines field-based forest science with advanced spatial technologies to generate operational and scientific knowledge for tropical forest management. His research has contributed to methodological advances that allow forest attributes, operational constraints, biomass stocks and canopy structure to be mapped with high spatial detail.

This technological and ecological perspective is directly aligned with GigaFlor’s mission to monitor Amazon giant forests through integrated protocols combining field plots, airborne LiDAR, satellite data, environmental monitoring and ecological modelling.
