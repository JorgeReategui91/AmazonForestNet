RESEARCH THEMES

Research Themes

Integrated science for understanding giant Amazon forests across field ecology, remote sensing, climate, dendrochronology, functional ecology and long-term monitoring.

SCIENTIFIC FRAMEWORK

Understanding giant forests across scales

The GigaFlor Amazon Giant Forests Observatory is organized around an integrated scientific framework designed to understand how giant tropical forests function, persist and respond to environmental change.

Our research combines field ecology, long-term monitoring, remote sensing, forest inventory, dendrochronology, environmental sensing and data science to investigate the ecological role of giant trees in the Amazon Basin.

By integrating permanent plots, airborne LiDAR, satellite observations, microclimatic monitoring, dendrometers, soil surveys, hydrological measurements, tree-ring records and ecological modelling, GigaFlor seeks to generate transformative knowledge on biodiversity, carbon storage, climate resilience and sustainable forest management.

THEMES

Core research themes

Nine interconnected themes structure the scientific identity of the GigaFlor observatory.

1. Giant Tree Ecology

Giant trees are among the most remarkable biological structures on Earth. Although relatively rare, they exert a disproportionate influence on forest architecture, biodiversity, carbon storage and ecosystem functioning.

This theme investigates the ecology, distribution, demography, regeneration and persistence of giant Amazonian trees, including individuals exceeding 60, 70 and 80 meters in height.

Key questions

  • Where do giant trees occur and why?
  • Which species reach exceptional dimensions?
  • How do giant trees influence biodiversity and forest structure?
  • What ecological processes occur after the fall of a giant tree?

2. Forest Carbon and Biomass

Large trees store a substantial proportion of tropical forest carbon. Understanding their contribution is critical for climate mitigation and conservation strategies.

This theme focuses on biomass estimation, carbon dynamics, allometric modelling and the identification of key carbon individuals.

Key questions

  • How much carbon is stored in giant trees?
  • How accurate are current biomass models for large trees?
  • Which species function as carbon hotspots?
  • How vulnerable are high-carbon forests to climate extremes?

3. Remote Sensing and Spatial Ecology

Remote sensing technologies are fundamental for monitoring giant forests across vast and remote regions of the Amazon.

This theme integrates airborne LiDAR, satellite imagery, UAV observations, GEDI products and spatial modelling to map forest structure, biomass, canopy height and giant-tree occurrence.

Key questions

  • How can LiDAR improve giant-tree detection?
  • Which environmental drivers determine giant-tree occurrence?
  • How can remote sensing support conservation planning?
  • What spatial patterns characterize giant-tree forests?

4. Climate Change and Forest Resilience

Amazonian forests are increasingly exposed to droughts, warming, altered precipitation regimes and extreme climatic events.

This theme investigates the vulnerability and resilience of giant trees under future climate scenarios by combining field monitoring, climate datasets, ecological forecasts and species distribution models.

Key questions

  • How sensitive are giant trees to drought?
  • Which climatic variables most influence their occurrence?
  • Where are future refugia for giant forests?
  • Can giant forests maintain ecological functions under climate stress?

5. Functional Ecology

Functional traits determine how trees acquire resources, tolerate stress and persist through time.

This theme investigates wood density, hydraulic architecture, leaf traits, crown structure and biomechanical strategies associated with giant-tree performance.

Key questions

  • Which traits characterize giant-tree species?
  • How do hydraulic systems influence tree performance?
  • What functional strategies promote exceptional growth?
  • Can functional traits improve ecological predictions?

6. Dendrochronology and Climate Reconstruction

Tree rings preserve valuable information about growth history, environmental variability and climate dynamics.

This theme integrates dendrochronology, dendroclimatology, wood anatomy and radiocarbon validation to reconstruct past climate conditions and understand growth trajectories of giant trees, with emphasis on Cedrela odorata.

Key questions

  • How old are giant Amazonian trees?
  • Can tree rings reconstruct past climate variability?
  • How do giant trees respond to droughts and floods?
  • How can tree-ring records improve climate projections?

7. Environmental Monitoring and Ecohydrology

The persistence of giant trees depends on environmental conditions operating above and below ground.

This theme investigates soil properties, hydrological processes, groundwater dynamics, topography and microclimate through continuous environmental monitoring.

Key questions

  • Which soil properties favor giant-tree growth?
  • How does groundwater availability influence forest structure?
  • What role does topography play in giant-tree distribution?
  • How does microclimate affect regeneration and performance?

8. Forest Dynamics and Regeneration

The fall of a giant tree creates major ecological disturbances that influence forest succession, biodiversity and biomass recovery.

This theme evaluates regeneration processes, seed banks, seedling communities, biomass accumulation and gap dynamics in forests affected by giant-tree mortality.

Key questions

  • How do giant-tree gaps influence regeneration?
  • Which species colonize large canopy openings?
  • How rapidly does biomass recover after disturbance?
  • Can giant-tree forests maintain long-term population viability?

9. Conservation, Bioeconomy and Socio-Ecological Systems

Giant forests are embedded within complex socio-ecological landscapes involving local communities, protected areas, public institutions and environmental governance.

This theme promotes the integration of scientific research with conservation strategies, environmental education, bioeconomy initiatives and sustainable development pathways throughout the Amazon.

Key questions

  • How can giant-tree science support conservation policy?
  • How can local communities participate in forest monitoring?
  • What bioeconomy opportunities are compatible with conservation?
  • How can science support decision-making in Amazonian landscapes?

SCIENTIFIC VISION

Toward a global reference in giant tropical forest science

Through these integrated research themes, GigaFlor seeks to become a global reference in the study of giant tropical forests, generating knowledge that advances ecological theory, informs conservation strategies and supports the sustainable future of Amazonian ecosystems.
